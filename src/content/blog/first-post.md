Welcome to JAM-Productions! We're excited to share our journey in software development with you. This first post is our introduction: who we are, how we work, and what we've built so far.

## Who We Are

We are a small group of developers who genuinely enjoy building things. Not in the abstract, "we love technology" sense, but in the concrete one: someone has an idea over a conversation, it sticks around for a few days, and a week later there's a repository with commits in it.

Our team brings together full stack and frontend developers, each with their own strengths, from React interfaces and design systems to backends, real-time infrastructure, and deployment. What we share is the desire to take an idea from a sketch to something you can actually open in a browser and use.

## Why We Build

Most of our projects start the same way: we wanted something to exist, and it didn't. Or it did, but not in the shape we wanted it.

That's a deliberate approach. Building for ourselves means we're our own first users, which keeps us honest about what actually matters. There's no roadmap handed down from anywhere, no feature written just to fill a slide. If something isn't fun to use, we notice immediately, because we're the ones using it.

It also means we get to be curious. Every project is an excuse to try a tool we haven't used before, a pattern we've only read about, or an architecture we want to understand properly rather than superficially. Shipping something real is, in our experience, the only way to learn a technology for good.

## What We Build

Our projects cover a wider range than you might expect from one team:

**Inkpostor** is a real-time multiplayer drawing and deduction game, built with React, TypeScript, and Socket.io. Real-time multiplayer forces you to think carefully about state, latency, and what happens when someone's connection drops mid-round.

**Foslog** brings reviews for films, series, video games, books, and music into one place, using Next.js and Supabase. It grew out of the frustration of keeping track of what we'd watched and read across half a dozen different apps.

**Malker** helps you organize a social plan with your friends, combining a React frontend with a Python and Flask backend on Firebase. It's the answer to the group chat where nobody ever settles on a time.

**Saurita** is a secretary bot for Discord, built with NodeJS. It takes care of the repetitive parts of running a server, the small chores nobody wants to do by hand twice.

**And more!** Beyond the projects featured here, there are plenty of other things we have built along the way: smaller tools, bots, and experiments that each scratched a specific itch. They all live in the repositories of our GitHub organization, open for anyone to browse.

Different problems, different stacks, same underlying instinct: see something worth making, then make it.

## How We Work

We work in the open. Everything we build lives on GitHub under the JAM-Productions organization, and we mean it when we say we like open source. Reading other people's code taught us most of what we know, and publishing ours is how we pay that back.

We favor small, complete projects over big, unfinished ones. A game like Inkpostor that friends can actually open and play tonight beats an ambitious platform that never leaves localhost. That preference shapes the way we scope things: get to something usable quickly, put it in front of real people, and iterate from there.

We also pick our tools per project rather than defaulting to the same stack every time. React and TypeScript show up often because they suit us, but Astro, Next.js, Flask, Supabase, and Firebase each earned their place by being the right fit for a specific problem.

## What's Next

This blog is where we'll write about that process: the technical decisions behind our projects, the things that went wrong and what we learned from them, and the occasional deep dive into a tool or pattern we've found genuinely useful.

Have a look at our projects on the home page, browse the code on GitHub, and if something we've built is useful to you, or broken for you, we'd like to hear about it.

Thanks for reading. There's more coming.
